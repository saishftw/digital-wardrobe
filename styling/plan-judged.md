# The judged plan — Bangkok / Phu Quoc / Hanoi, 17–24 Aug 2026

Final. Built by judgement, not by the optimiser, then revised against the
wearer's own calls. Where this differs from `packing-list.md`, this file wins.

## Where the pipeline was wrong

1. **The `+12` pick bonus dominates.** Scores span 85–120, so a single grade
   outweighs every material, colour and climate term combined. The optimiser
   can only re-rank combos you already blessed; it cannot propose.
2. **No concept of putting a good piece at risk.** It sent the Striped Camp
   Collar kayaking at Halong because linen scores well on quick-dry — ignoring
   that you do not take your best shirt into a kayak. Same error at the
   go-kart track, where it put beige linen next to chain grease.
3. **No sun or sweat model.** It put Navy Striped in direct midday sun at the
   Grand Palace. Navy absorbs heat and shows sweat; that walk is 3+ hours on
   open stone at 33 °C.
4. **No mosquito model.** Phu Quoc, August, evening, feet in sand — that is a
   long-sleeve slot, and nothing in the scoring says so.
5. **Laundry is invisible to it.** It spaces re-wears by "3 slots" regardless of
   the Aug 19 Bangkok and Aug 22 Hanoi laundry drops, which reset everything.
6. **The flight home is not a fashion slot.** Aug 24 you see nobody. It should
   absorb the most-worn items, and the optimiser scored it like any other day.
7. **It never used the Sambas.** 14/14 slots in cork sandals made them dead
   weight — but six slots genuinely want a closed shoe.
8. **It treated the two Ta Hien nights as identical.** Aug 23 is the Saturday
   street closure and is the bigger night; Aug 22 is an arrival evening.
9. **It had no venue-lighting term.** Navy goes flat and muddy under the warm
   sodium light of a night market. It is a daylight colour.

## The backup outfit

**Wine Polo + Black Pleated Wide + Sambas, polo fully tucked.**

Not in the rotation, but it costs nothing to know it is in the bag — both
pieces are already packed. Deploy it if Lebua turns you away in the rustic;
sky-bar doors are inconsistent, and this is the strongest fallback you own.

It is out of the rotation on **opportunity cost, not merit**: Wine Polo +
Pleated scores 86, but Wine Polo + Beige Linen scores 104, and the pleated has
two better tops. Both pieces have a better partner available.

**Rules if you wear it:**

- **Full tuck, always.** The pleated is a high rise; untucked, the polo hem
  falls below the natural waist, hides the rise and kills the entire
  proportional benefit. No French tuck — that is for curved shirttails, not a
  straight-hem polo.
- **Belt, dark brown leather.** A tucked polo over an exposed waistband looks
  unfinished. Brown keeps the warm thread running; black flattens it.
- **One button undone, not two.** Two gapes on a knit placket and reads resort.

Why it works at all: wine is the colour at your face and it is in palette, the
black stays below the waist so the warm-autumn-and-black problem never arises,
the wide pleated leg drapes past the midsection instead of gripping it, and the
open polo placket makes the small V a round face wants. Its one weakness is
register — pique knit is the least formal of the three pleated pairings.

## The calls I would defend hardest

**Rustic + Pleated at Lebua.** Lebua is the only venue on the trip with a
published, enforced door code, and the one you are most likely to be
photographed at. Rust on black reads deliberate; a woven button-down clears a
door that a knit polo only might.

**Camp Collar + Pleated at Tribe / Aether.** Slub texture and vertical stripes
carry a club better than flat pique, and this keeps the pleated on two
consecutive nights — which is what finally justifies packing it. Poly-viscose
does not need a wash in between.

**White Crew at the Grand Palace and the go-kart track.** The Palace requires
covered shoulders and long trousers, nothing more, so a crew-neck clears it.
White reflects three hours of open stone at 33 °C, and the chino takes kart
abuse in a way wide linen never would. If you would rather not risk white near
chain grease, Navy Striped + Camel is also green-flagged — you pay for it in
heat and visible sweat.

**Rust at Sunset Town.** Warm-lit outdoor show and night market. Rust against
beige is the warmest, most on-palette thing you can assemble, and navy would go
flat under that lighting. This puts rust on Aug 18 and Aug 20 — two nights
apart with the Aug 19 morning laundry between them, so **that laundry drop is
load-bearing.** If the hotel cannot turn it around same day, fall back to Navy
Striped (SS) + Beige Shorts.

**No white on a beer street.** Plastic stools, grilled food, spilled bia hơi and
motorbikes at ankle height. Aug 22 gets the wine polo, which hides everything;
Aug 23 — the bigger Saturday night, after the Aug 22 evening Hanoi laundry —
gets the camp collar with black shorts.

**Long sleeves, not trousers, at OCSEN.** Feet-in-the-sand bar, so trousers are
wrong whatever the mosquitoes are doing. Sleeves plus repellent is the answer.
The White LS is heavily constrained anyway — you vetoed it with beige linen,
beige shorts, camel and brown chinos, so it lives with black or navy shorts.

**Jäger tee on the water, both days.** Its job is not to win an outfit, it is to
take the salt, chlorine and sun so the camp collar does not. A wet woven shirt
in a kayak flaps in the paddle stroke and catches on the cockpit rim, and white
cotton goes transparent when soaked and yellows permanently from sunscreen.

**Camel is the travel trouser on all four flights.** Wrinkle 6 against the beige
linen's 3, and laundry on Aug 19 and Aug 22 means Aug 24 flies on a clean pair.
Beige linen never sees an airport.
